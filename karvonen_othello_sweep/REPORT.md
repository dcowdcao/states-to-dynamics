# Karvonen's OthelloGPT SAE sweep: the complete record

All 320 SAEs he trained on layer 5 of OthelloGPT, five trainer types -- 280 that appear in his
published results CSV and 40 TopK checkpoints he evaluated but never published. Metrics come
from each checkpoint's own `results.csv`, hyperparameters from its `config.json`, coverage
recomputed from its saved per-feature counts with his own code. Regenerated by
`build_report.py`; no number below was typed by hand.

**Which do we trust?** For the board columns the question barely arises: the two runs agree to a
median 0.00019 relative on reconstruction F1, worst case 0.0052 absolute, and they score the
identical evaluation set (`num_squares` and `board_reconstruction_board_count` are bit-identical
on all 280 shared rows). For the SAE-quality columns neither run is more trustworthy than the
other, because **both are single-batch estimates**: Karvonen computes l0, FVE, `frac_alive`,
`cossim` and `l2_ratio` from one batch of 25 activations. Their disagreement is therefore a
direct measurement of that noise -- median 0.065 relative on l0, up to 51 absolute on the
densest rows -- not evidence that one file is wrong. This report takes the per-checkpoint value
as primary (it is the run the 40 new rows come from, so the TopK sweep stays internally
consistent) and keeps the aggregate's l0 alongside it in `sweep_full.csv` as `l0_published`.

**Coverage is not in the per-checkpoint files.** They carry 84 columns against the aggregate's
435; what is lost is `best_average_f1` (coverage) for all three board functions, its argmax
index, the serialised `all_average_f1s` vector, and 342 `f1_threshold_*` columns. What is gained
is `best_f1_score_per_square`, six columns the aggregate does not have. Since coverage is the
metric this report ranks on, it is **recomputed** for all 320 checkpoints by
`recompute_coverage.py`, which runs Karvonen's own `analysis.get_all_f1s` +
`f1_analysis.best_f1_average` over each checkpoint's saved counts.

That recomputation is a reproduction, not a re-estimate. Across all 280 published rows it
reproduces his coverage column to a median 6e-08 and a maximum 0.00039 -- more than an order of
magnitude tighter than the run-to-run spread on reconstruction F1 (0.0052).
`f1_analysis.py:159-232` shows why: his coverage columns were themselves produced by loading
these same pickles, so the published aggregate is a mix -- SAE-quality and reconstruction
columns from the 2024-06-10 re-evaluation, coverage columns derived from the 2024-05-27 saved
counts. **Coverage for the 40 unpublished rows therefore stands on exactly the same footing as
his published coverage.**

## What the four metrics are

Karvonen scores each SAE against three board-state functions. Two are used here; the third,
`games_batch_to_valid_moves`, is summarised at the end.

- `mine_yours` -- each square carries one of 3 classes (blank / mine / yours): 64 x 3 = 192
  board-state properties.
- `mine_yours_blank_mask` -- the same, scored only on squares that actually hold a disc.

For each function, two numbers, both taken after an internal sweep over 11 activation
thresholds:

- **coverage** (`best_average_f1`, `circuits/f1_analysis.py:14-28`): for each of the 192
  properties, take the F1 of the single best feature for it; average over properties. "Does the
  dictionary contain a dedicated feature for each thing the board can be doing?" Karvonen's own
  plot axis calls this *Coverage*.
- **board reconstruction F1** (`best_f1_score_per_class`,
  `circuits/eval_board_reconstruction.py:307-331`): OR together the labels of every
  simultaneously-active feature to rebuild the board, then micro-F1 over all (board, square,
  class) cells. "Can you read the board off the dictionary?"

Both come from 1000 games x 59 positions and are solid. They measure different things and they
do not agree: across the 286 surviving SAEs their Spearman correlation is -0.3.

## Read this before you rank anything

**1. The SAE-quality columns are single-batch estimates; the board columns are not.** Karvonen
computes l0, FVE, `frac_alive`, `cossim` and `l2_ratio` from one batch of 25 activations, and
his two runs disagree on l0 by a median 0.065 relative (51 of 280 rows identical -- the TopK
rows, where L0 == k by construction, plus the dead rows where it is 0). So treat "matched L0" as
matched to within about 10%, not exactly: of the 73 configs in the matched band, 14 sit within
10% of an edge and could fall the other side under the aggregate's numbers. Nothing in the
recommendation turns on those.

FVE is worse than noisy at the sparse end. The two runs agree to a median 0.013 absolute above
L0 100 and 0.036 between L0 20 and 100, but on the 34 rows below L0 5 they disagree by up to
0.76 -- one run reports 0.8255 where the other reports 0.1751 for the same checkpoint. FVE at
very low L0 is not comparable across runs and is not used for anything here, including the
aliveness cut, which is thresholded on L0 instead (5; collapsed rows top out at 2.75 and the
next surviving row is at 10, and that split is identical under both runs).

The **board metrics carry no such caveat**: 1000 games x 59 positions, and the two runs agree to
a median 0.00019 relative.

**2. 34 of the 320 rows are collapsed, not merely sparse.** All are `StandardTrainer`, all from
the original sweep, all at l1_penalty >= 0.1716. The original othello L1 grid ran to 0.9 while
the corrected one stops at 0.08, so from the fourth grid point on the SAE trains to nothing. 11
have L0 and `frac_variance_explained` exactly 0. They are flagged `collapsed` in
`sweep_full.csv` and excluded from every selection here. Note their `num_alive_features` is
still 37-269: that column does not go to zero on a dead SAE.

**3. The blank-masked coverage column sits on a different scale from the unmasked one.**
`circuits/othello_utils.py:119` zeroes the *blank class channel* of the labels, but
`othello_mine_yours_blank_mask_config` (`circuits/chess_utils.py:772-778`) leaves
`one_hot_mask_idx` at `None`, and that field is the only thing that would shrink the denominator
in `f1_analysis.py:21-24`. So all 64 blank-class F1 entries are identically zero while the mean
is still taken over 192 entries. The column is mechanically capped at 128/192 = 0.6667 and its
absolute value is **not** comparable to the unmasked column. (The chess masked configs *do* set
`one_hot_mask_idx=6` and are unaffected.) Two consequences: the cap is the same constant on
every row, so **ranking within the column is untouched**; and multiplying by 1.5 restores the
unmasked scale, which is the `cov bm x1.5` column -- it lands within 0.035 of unmasked coverage
on average across surviving rows, the check that the constant is right.

**4. `-v2` rows are a second sweep, and the two grids overlap at the seams.** On 2024-05-26 the
standard and gated grids were re-run: standard `linspace(0.035, 0.9, 20)` -> `linspace(0.035,
0.08, 20)`, gated `linspace(0.2, 1.0, 20)` -> `linspace(1.0, 2.0, 20)`. p_anneal and
gated_anneal never were, which is why only those two types carry `-v2` rows. The configs confirm
it directly -- `trainer19-v2` records l1_penalty 0.08 against `trainer1`'s 0.08053 -- and the
grids share endpoints, so a few configs appear twice at different seeds:

| overlapping config (dict 4096) | orig dir  | L0    | -v2 dir      | L0    |
|--------------------------------|-----------|-------|--------------|-------|
| standard, l1 = 0.035           | trainer0  | 399.6 | trainer0-v2  | 396.5 |
| standard, l1 ~ 0.08            | trainer1  | 66.94 | trainer19-v2 | 67.26 |
| gated, l1 = 1.0                | trainer19 | 127.7 | trainer0-v2  | 125.8 |

Treat those as replicate pairs; do not concatenate the two sweeps into one grid.

**5. `frac_recovered` is saturated and ranks nothing.** Across the 286 surviving SAEs it spans
0.731337 to 1.0001, with 199 of them within 0.001 of 1.000, and it comes from the same single
batch. Do not put it in a selection rule.

**6. `frac_alive` is not the fraction of live features.** On every non-TopK row it equals
exactly `l0 / dict_size` -- the mean fraction of the dictionary active *per token*, mislabelled.
On every TopK row it equals exactly `1 / dict_size`, a constant. Use `num_alive_features`.

## Context: the floor and the neuron bar

Control rows come from Karvonen's published control CSVs, so they are on the aggregate run.

| best SAE, by reconstruction F1            | 429.4 | 5397       | 0.4996   | 0.361  | 0.5415      | 0.9522   | 0.9543      |

*The floor is high.* SAEs trained on a **randomly initialised** OthelloGPT reach coverage 0.4316
and reconstruction F1 0.4015. Any claim about board representations has to clear that, not zero.
(That sweep is standard-trainer only and inherits the same broken L1 grid, so 27 of its 40 rows
are dead; the row above is the best survivor.)

*No SAE in the sweep beats raw neurons on coverage.* Layer-5 MLP neurons -- 2048 of them, read
directly, L0 1864 -- reach coverage 0.8037, above the best SAE (0.7704). On reconstruction F1
the best SAE (0.9522) does beat the neurons (0.8807). Caveat: the neurons are the MLP hidden
layer while the SAEs sit on the residual stream, so this is a bar rather than a controlled
ablation -- but it is the bar that matters when the pitch is "the dictionary is more
interpretable than the neurons".

## Which criterion, and why

**Karvonen has no composite selection score.** In his repo every `best_*` is an argmax *within*
one SAE (over the 11 activation thresholds, or over features); the only cross-config ranking
anywhere is a `print_top_n_values(df, key, n)` in `circuits/f1_analysis.ipynb` that sorts by one
metric column at a time to quote maxima in the paper's results text. Every figure is a scatter
of one metric against L0, coloured by trainer class, with the trade-off left to the reader's
eye. Inventing a weighted score would be putting our thumb on his scale, so we do not.

That leaves choosing a criterion and defending it. The recommendation uses **blank-masked
coverage at matched dictionary size and matched L0**:

*Coverage over reconstruction F1*, because reconstruction F1 is a decodability test a dense
uninterpretable basis passes. It is maximised at the densest end of the grid (rho(L0, recon F1)
= 0.9822 for standard SAEs) and its sweep-wide winner has near-floor coverage. Coverage asks the
question a dictionary exists to answer: is there one feature per board-state property.

| type                   | rho(L0, coverage) | rho(L0, recon F1) | rho(coverage, recon F1) | n alive |
|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|---------|
| Standard               | 0.8247            | 0.9822            | 0.7462                  | 46      |
| Standard + p-annealing | 0.9675            | -0.1317           | -0.2077                 | 40      |
| Gated                  | -0.2382           | -0.8431           | 0.3849                  | 80      |
| Gated + p-annealing    | -0.8041           | -0.9527           | 0.8043                  | 40      |
| TopK                   | 0.02317           | -0.8804           | 0.2407                  | 80      |

*Blank-masked over unmasked*, because "this square is empty" is the cheap third of the unmasked
label set -- early-game boards are mostly empty and blankness is close to a function of move
count. Removing it is the entire point of the masked config. The masked column's denominator bug
(trap 3) rescales every row by one constant and so cannot distort a ranking, and the switch does
change the answer at the top: unmasked coverage puts TopK first, blank-masked puts Gated first.

*Matched dict 8192 and matched L0 60-200*, because the SpaDE study trains every architecture at
dict 8192 and evaluates its TopK and BatchTopK baselines near L0 80, and because the families'
grids do not span the same sparsity range -- an unconstrained per-type maximum partly rewards
whichever grid happened to reach the most favourable L0.

**What this does not license.** These are Karvonen's numbers from his evaluation code. The SpaDE
study re-implements coverage and board reconstruction with a train/test split for the
feature-to-property assignment and a 60-square (non-middle) mask, and retrains its own
baselines. At a nominally identical config -- TopK, dict 8192, L0 80 -- these files give
coverage 0.6627 and reconstruction F1 0.9019, which the study's own run of the same trainer does
not reproduce. Use this sweep to pick *hyperparameters to retrain and re-evaluate in house*; do
not paste these numbers into a table beside SpaDE's.

## Provenance

Every hyperparameter is **read from the released checkpoint's `config.json`**, joined on (group
dir, trainer dir); `build_report.py` raises if any row lacks one. The released standard configs
omit `activation_dim`, `dict_size` and `dict_class`, so for those 80 checkpoints the dictionary
size is measured from `ae.pt`'s `encoder.weight` shape. The recorded field is `l1_penalty` for
standard and gated, `sparsity_penalty` for the two p-annealed types (the sweep script calls the
same variable `initial_sparsity_penalty_`), and `k` for TopK.

**Cross-check of the index-decoding rule.** An earlier pass inferred `trainer index =
expansion_index * S + sparsity_index` with S = 20 for the four penalty trainers and S = 40 for
top_k, expansion factors [8, 16] at activation dim 512, over the grids in trap 4. Against the
config files that rule is **correct everywhere**: no dictionary-size mismatch on any of the 320
checkpoints, and the largest disagreement in a sparsity value is 1.2e-07, float32 grid rounding.
